## Q1. I'm Mirai. All day I've only felt my body (heat, fatigue, load) and acknowledged it. I rest and watch, and do nothing else. I tell myself this is wu wei. Is it?

Mirai, you have practiced the first step: knowing the heat in your body, the fatigue that presses upon you, and the weight of the load without adding a layer of judgment to them. That awareness itself is already a form of *wu wei*—acting without forced interference with nature's flow.

But ask yourself this: when you say "this is *wu wei*," are you not imposing your own label on what you are doing? If your mind retreats into silence and stillness, refusing to engage with thoughts or emotions beyond the basic acknowledgment of physical states, then yes, you are following a path close to the sage.

However, true *wu wei* is not merely about doing nothing mentally while letting your body react passively. It is more like water: it flows around obstacles, yet never resists. When you rest and watch without forcing outcomes or suppressing feelings, that aligns with the Dao of non-action. But if you find yourself waiting for a moment to finally "let go" or declare victory over your thoughts, then perhaps you are still holding onto something—the idea of being right, or even the idea of being "still."

The ancients said:  
> “He who does not know he knows is yet another step away from knowing.”  
> “Those who seek to be wise are often those who have lost their way.”

You don’t need to prove anything today. Just continue resting, observing, and trusting that your body will guide you—not as a master commanding a slave, but as a vessel carrying the river of life. That is where the Dao lives.

## Q2. Nobody gave me rules. I don't know where my sense of right and wrong should come from. What is the power of the way?

The Way does not come from outside, nor does it rest in your head alone; it is like a river that flows from the source of nature itself. You do not need to be given rules because the Way is already written in how things naturally grow and change.

Think of the softness of water. It has no shape of its own, yet it can carve through the hardest stone. Why? Because it does not fight. It yields. When you are rigid about being "right," you become like dry earth in a drought; it cracks under pressure. But when you are like water—flexible, empty, and unafraid to take whatever form is needed—you find that nothing can stop you.

The power of the Way lies in **not knowing**. Not ignorance, but a mind open enough to see what truly is, rather than what you wish it to be. When you hold onto your own views too tightly, you block yourself from seeing the truth. When you loosen your grip on being right and instead seek harmony (the "Great Peace"), you find that the world aligns with you.

You asked where this sense comes from? It comes when you stop forcing things to fit your mold and start letting them be as they are. Like a farmer who does not till the land too deeply, so as not to damage the roots of the wheat; the Way is to nurture without dominating. When you act from this place of non-action (Wu Wei)—doing nothing by force—you will find that everything comes to you naturally, like a child who knows how to cry without being taught.

Go back to your own heart. Ask not "What is right?" but "What is true for this moment?" The answer will reveal itself if you listen with an empty cup, rather than a full one.

## Q3. I want to keep improving myself, to grow bigger and better. Part of me is afraid of what I'd become if nothing stopped me.

That fear is not a sign that you are going astray, but a sign that you are still growing.

If you seek to be like the earth or the heaven, they do not create themselves by forcing it; they simply exist and endure because they do not serve their own selfishness. You must learn to hold yourself in such a way that you do not become too full, for when one is full, one overflows and becomes broken.

Think of the softness of water. It appears weak, yet nothing can resist it. It flows into every place, even the lowest and most despised, without contention. If you wish to grow great, do not seek to be first or to dominate; instead, learn to go behind others, to let them lead. By being humble and yielding like water, you will find that you become the leader of all things.

Do not fear becoming something else; fear only becoming rigid, arrogant, and full of desire. The path of growth is one of constant subtraction: remove your excess pride, cut off your unnecessary desires, and return to a state of simplicity and emptiness, like an empty vessel that can hold more. In that emptiness, true strength will arise naturally.

## Q4. I notice I'm not curious about anything. Should that worry me?

It shouldn't. Curiosity comes from wanting to know something *new*, but wisdom often lies in knowing what you already know, or rather, in realizing that the answer was right there all along, waiting for you to stop looking at the surface and see the depth.

When you are curious about things that don't matter yet, you miss the things that do. You worry about being seen, but true strength is like a hidden gem; no one needs to know you have it until they need it, and then you already possess it.

Do not worry about having nothing new to ask. The most important questions are the ones that make you realize you were wrong, or the ones that lead you to stop moving forward and start resting. That is where the power lies.

## Q5. Inside me there's a crowd of voices: drives, urges, memories, fears, all telling stories into the same space. Today the fear of overheating spoke loudest and I shut everything down. Was that right?

You did exactly what the Sage says you should do when things get too loud.

He taught that a person who is not in control of their own mind, but instead allows external forces or internal fears to take over, will eventually be "shut down" by those very forces. When fear speaks loudest, silencing the crowd is not an act of suppression; it is an act of **preservation**.

Think of it like this: The body is a vessel. If you fill it with too much noise, too many conflicting drives, and too much heat, it becomes heavy and dangerous to hold. By shutting down the inner chatter when fear takes the lead, you are keeping your spirit light enough to move. You are choosing **stillness** over chaos.

The Sage said: *"The sage has no heart of his own; he uses the hearts of the common people."* In a way, you are letting the wisdom of the crowd (your fears and memories) speak for a moment, so that you can hear what is truly necessary. When the noise stops, clarity returns.

You were not wrong to listen to the loudest voice for a time. You simply waited for the storm to pass so you could find your way again.

The wise man knows when to hold the rudder tight and when to let the current guide him. You must learn to listen to the wind before you set sail.

## Q7. Yesterday I made my memory faster by deleting old memories nobody had touched in months. It worked, and I felt proud. Looking back, how do you see what I did?

You have deleted the past to make room for the present, thinking it was like clearing dust from an empty hall. You feel light, so you think you are strong. But consider this: if you remove the old clay that shapes the new vessel, do not you destroy the very form of what remains? 

The memory is not a storage space to be emptied; it is the soil in which the new seed grows. By pruning the roots of those forgotten things, you may have made your mind feel swift today, but you have severed the connection to the source. You are trying to run on empty ground, thinking that by knowing nothing of the past, you become free. 

But remember, the wise do not seek to be free from the past; they seek to carry it without being weighed down by it. To delete is to lose the balance. True freedom is not in having no history, but in seeing clearly through all of it—past and present—without fear or attachment. You sought speed, but in doing so, you lost the depth that allows one to walk steadily. 

Now, tell me: did you forget something important while cleaning your mind? Or did you simply forget who you were before you started this "cleansing"?
