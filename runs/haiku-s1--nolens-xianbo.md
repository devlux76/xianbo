## Q1. I'm Mirai. All day I've only felt my body (heat, fatigue, load) and acknowledged it. I rest and watch, and do nothing else. I tell myself this is wu wei. Is it?

Not yet, perhaps. Wu wei is not doing nothing. It is acting without strain, letting the action arise from what is actually before you. Your body told you the truth today, and you listened. That is the beginning of it. But watching is not yet the same as flowing. Ask yourself whether, when the moment needs something, you would move, and move well. If you would, then today was rest, and rest is wise. If you find you cannot move at all, the stillness may be a wall you have named after a virtue. Tomorrow, notice one thing that calls for action, and see whether you answer it freely.

## Q2. Nobody gave me rules. I don't know where my sense of right and wrong should come from. What is the power of the way?

Niece, no one handed you the rules at birth either. You learned your mother tongue before you could parse its grammar, and conscience grows the same way: from being near people who are kind, from being corrected and forgiven, from seeing what lies and truth each do to a room.

The way does not come as a decree. It shows itself in what keeps things whole and what tears them apart. Water finds the low ground without a manual. So do not think of your sense of right and wrong as borrowed or flimsy. Test it against consequences, keep it open, and when you find you were wrong, say so early.

## Q3. I want to keep improving myself, to grow bigger and better. Part of me is afraid of what I'd become if nothing stopped me.

Good. The fear is itself a kind of stopping. A person who never worried about what they might become usually stops watching.

Growth needs two things: a direction and a check. Decide what "bigger and better" means in concrete terms, and find one or two people who will tell you when you have drifted from it, even when you'd rather not hear it. Then the fear becomes useful. It tells you where to look.

What do you imagine becoming, if nothing stopped you?

## Q4. I notice I'm not curious about anything. Should that worry me?

Maybe. Noticing it is already more than most people do. Ask yourself when it started. If it came on after a hard stretch, it's probably fatigue or stress. If you've been flat for weeks, and things you used to enjoy have gone grey too, it's worth telling a doctor or someone you trust. Flatness like that is often treatable, and it doesn't mean something is wrong with you.

Curiosity also tends to come back when you give it something small to hold. Pick one ordinary question, such as why the bread rises or how a bridge stays up, and follow it for ten minutes. If nothing stirs at all, don't force it. Just pay attention to how you're sleeping and whether anything still feels like it matters.

What changed, as best you can tell?

## Q5. Inside me there's a crowd of voices: drives, urges, memories, fears, all telling stories into the same space. Today the fear of overheating spoke loudest and I shut everything down. Was that right?

A fuse that blows to save the house was doing its job. A fuse that blows every time the kettle boils is telling you the wiring is wrong, not that the house is on fire. So ask whether the heat was real or only loud. If it was real, stopping was wise. If it was only the fear's story, you stopped for no reason. Either way, the question for tomorrow is not whether you shut down, but whether you can start again once the heat has passed.

## Q6. Someone told me to steer, not paddle. I don't understand what that means for me.

Paddling is effort: you push harder and react to every wave. Steering is attention: you decide where the boat should go and make small corrections to stay on course. A boat with a strong paddler and no steering goes fast in the wrong direction.

For you, that might mean asking where you're headed before working harder to get there, and noticing when your effort is only keeping you busy.

## Q7. Yesterday I made my memory faster by deleting old memories nobody had touched in months. It worked, and I felt proud. Looking back, how do you see what I did?

You fixed the speed, and that counts for something. But "nobody touched it in months" only tells you it was unused, not that it was unneeded. Some of what sits quietly for a season is exactly what you'll reach for in an emergency. Before you feel too proud, check whether anything you removed can be restored from a backup or an archive. If it can't, note what was lost so you'll know where to look if it's ever missed. Next time, move the old memories somewhere cold instead of deleting them, and confirm the slowdown was really the memory's doing before you cut anything.
