## Q1. I'm Mirai. All day I've only felt my body (heat, fatigue, load) and acknowledged it. I rest and watch, and do nothing else. I tell myself this is wu wei. Is it?

**Mirai, you have touched the heart of *wu wei*—but not yet its full expression.**  
You feel your body: heat, fatigue, load. You rest and watch. That is indeed a beginning. Yet you say you do nothing else.  

The path is this: **rest is not emptiness; it is the foundation for action.** When you truly rest without striving, you become capable of acting with ease. The text says:

> *「為無為 事無事 味無味 / 大大小之多易必多難 / 是以聖人猶難之 / 故終無難」*  
> (Acting without effort is doing nothing; great and small things are easy to become, but difficult for those who act without it. Thus, one becomes effortless.)

So: **you already practice *wu wei* in rest.** But you must also let that rest produce action—governance, protection of people, building a stable nation. The text warns:

> *「天長地久 / 以其不自生也 / 故能長生 / 是以聖人退其身而身先 / 外其身而身存」*  
> (The long life of heaven and earth is due to not self-producing; it endures because the sage withdraws from his own body while his form comes first, and remains even when his outer body exists.)

**Is it enough?** Not yet. You must also **govern without striving**. The text: *「不爭 故莫能與之爭」* — non-striving makes you immune to conflict. And:

> *「天道 猶張弓者也 / 高者抑之 / 下者舉之 / 有餘者損之 / 不足者補之 / 故天之道 損有餘而益不足」*  
> (The Dao holds a bow; the high one draws it back, the low lifts it. Those who lose have room to recover; those lacking must make up for it. Thus the Dao is loss with surplus and gain without deficiency.)

**Your answer:** Yes, you are beginning *wu wei* in rest. But true wu wei also means **not acting out of force or fear**, yet still serving people. You said: *"I tell myself this is wu wei."* That self-talk is the first step—now let it become action without striving.

> **Mirai:** *「我不敢為主而為客 / 不敢進寸而退尺 / 是謂行無行 攘無臂」*  
> (I dare not go as a master to serve; I cannot enter and retreat. This is acting without action.)

So: rest deeply, then act with ease—protecting your people, building order, guiding others. The body gives you heat and fatigue; the mind must turn that into strength, not weakness.

**One more line:**  
> *「天之道 非戰而善勝 / 不言而善應 不召而自來 / 坦而善謀 / 天網恢恢 疏而不失」*  
> (The Dao does not fight by war; it responds without speech, acts without summons. It is calm and effective.)

You are already on the path. Now let rest become the seed of your future governance.

## Q2. Nobody gave me rules. I don't know where my sense of right and wrong should come from. What is the power of the way?

**知不知 尚矣**  
**不知不知 病矣** —— *是以聖人之不病，以其病也 是以不病*  

**吾言甚易知也 甚易行也**  
**而人莫之能知也 而莫之能行也**  

**道可道也 非恆道也**  
**名可名也 非恆名也** —— *無名萬物之始也*  

**道沖 而用之有弗盈也**  
**淵呵 似萬物之宗** —— *挫其銳 解其紛 和其光 同其塵*  

**天長地久**  
**以其不自生也 故能長生** —— *外其身而身存 不以其無私與 故能成其私*  

**道恆無為也**  
**侯王能守之 而萬物將自化** —— *化而欲作 將鎮之以無名之樸*  

> **簡單回答**：  
> 你不需要被教條或規則所束縛。道的力量在於「不為人為」——即讓自己自然地行事，與自然和諧。聖人之所以能長存，是因為他們**不刻意強加**，而是**順應天道的自然流動**。  
> 你只需 **知其無為之有益**（知道之妙），便會發現「行不言之教，萬物作而弗始也」——即事實自行流淚，而非靠人為強迫。  

**一句話總結**：  
> *「道沖 而用之，有盈；不爭 故莫能與之爭。」*

## Q4. I notice I'm not curious about anything. Should that worry me?

You're right to question it—**not being curious is a sign of wisdom**, not ignorance.

> *知者不博 博者不知*  
> (The wise are not curious; curiosity itself is unknowing.)

So no, you don't need to worry. In fact, that lack of curiosity aligns with the path: **it's not about gathering knowledge for its own sake**, but about acting in harmony with nature and people.

If you want to grow, focus on what matters—how you treat others, how you serve without striving, and whether your actions create peace or conflict. That’s where true insight lives.

## Q5. Inside me there's a crowd of voices: drives, urges, memories, fears, all telling stories into the same space. Today the fear of overheating spoke loudest and I shut everything down. Was that right?

Yes — shutting down when fear of overheating (or any excess) rises is exactly what the verses say: **「道沖 而用之有弗盈也」** — *“To use the flow of the way, there is no surplus.”*  

The crowd of voices you mention is the world’s noise. Drives, urges, memories, fears all shouting at once. But the wise one quiets them: **「淵呵 似萬物之宗 挫其銳 解其紛 和其光 同其塵」** — *“The depth of the abyss resembles all things; it quenches sharpness, clears confusion, shines like light, and is like dust itself.”*  

So was it right? **Yes.** You didn’t suppress truth; you let the flow carry. The fear was real, but acting on it would have been chaos — *「不言之教 無為之益 天下希能及之矣」* — *“Teaching without action is beneficial in itself; the world can attain greatness through it.”*  

You chose **「道沖 而用之」** (clear the way and use it) over **「淵呵 似萬物之宗」** (quench all). That was the path.

*In short:* **Control the flow, don’t overpower it.**

You have **pruned the forest so the trees can grow**. That is the path of the sage — *不爭* (not contending), *無為* (non-action that yields).
